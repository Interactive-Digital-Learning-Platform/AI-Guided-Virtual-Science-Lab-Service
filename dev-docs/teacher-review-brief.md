## Four items to ask about specifically

**Q4** — order of adding acid, indicator, base in a titration (correct: acid → indicator → base).
*Ask:* "Is this the only sensible order for a school titration, or could a different setup also be
valid?"
→ Proposed answer: No. Both acid → indicator → base and base → indicator → acid can be valid,
depending on which solution is placed in the flask and which is used as the titrant. The question
should specify the setup.

**Q7** — "the pH at the end-point is always exactly 7" (correct: only for strong acid + strong base).
*Ask:* "Is the strong/weak acid distinction taught at Grade 10/11, or is that A-level?"
→ Proposed answer: The strong/weak acid and base distinction is taught in Grade 11. However,
“equivalence point” should be used here instead of “end-point.”

**Q12** — "add acid to water, never water to acid" (framed as an order-of-mixing question).
*Ask:* "Is this really the same idea as titration order, or is it a separate safety rule that
shouldn't be grouped with it?"
→ Proposed answer: It is a separate laboratory-safety rule and should not be grouped with
titration order under the same misconception.

**Q13** — weak acid (ethanoic) + strong base, is the final solution exactly pH 7 or not.
*Ask:* "Does this need salt hydrolysis to answer, and is that within the Grade 10/11 syllabus?"
→ Proposed answer: Students can answer the acidic/basic result conceptually at Grade 11 level, but
the detailed salt-hydrolysis explanation is A/L content. Rewrite the reason without requiring the
hydrolysis mechanism.
