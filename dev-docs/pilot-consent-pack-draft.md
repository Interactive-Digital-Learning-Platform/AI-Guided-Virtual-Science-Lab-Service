> **Status: template only — not a confirmed, approvable consent instrument.** Every bracketed

> ethics/IRB reference, school, and contact details. Nobody should be asked to sign this until

> they're independent gates — so it can move forward in parallel.

## What this pack is for

The acid-base misconception study needs 15–20 Grade 10/11 students to: (1) do one practical in
the app with research logging on, and (2) answer a short two-tier paper quiz before and after.
Participants are minors, so both a parent/guardian's consent and the student's own assent are
needed before anyone is recruited.

## Part A — Parent / Guardian Consent Form

**Study title:** [working title — e.g. "A computer model that notices common acid-base
misunderstandings during a virtual chemistry practical"]

**Student researcher:** [your full name], [your program / year], [your university]
**Supervisor:** [supervisor's full name and contact email]
**Ethics/approval reference:** [fill in once obtained — leave blank until then, do not invent one]

**What this is:** Your child is invited to take part in a short study about an educational app
used for a virtual chemistry practical (acid-base neutralisation). The study is part of a
final-year university research project.

**What participation involves:**
- Your child answers a short written quiz (about 10 minutes) before the practical.
- Your child completes one virtual chemistry practical in the app (about 20–30 minutes), as they
  would normally.
- Your child answers a second short written quiz (about 10 minutes) after the practical.
- While your child uses the app, the researcher's software records the steps they take on the
  virtual bench (e.g. which chemicals and equipment they use, in what order) so a computer model
  can be checked against real student behaviour. **No audio, video, or photo of your child is
  recorded.**

**What is NOT affected:** Participation (or not) has no effect on your child's grades, standing
at school, or ongoing use of the app for normal schoolwork.

**Risks:** No more than normal classroom computer use. The chemistry in the app is simulated —
no real chemicals are involved.

**Confidentiality:**
- Your child's name is never stored with their quiz answers or bench-action data. Each
  participant is assigned a code (e.g. "P07") known only to the researcher; the link between the
  code and your child's name is kept separately and destroyed once the study is written up.
- Data is stored on [where — e.g. "a password-protected university-managed database"] and only
  the researcher and supervisor can access it.
- Results are reported only as group statistics (e.g. "12 of 17 students...") — never
  individually, and never in a way that could identify your child.

**Voluntary participation:** Taking part is entirely voluntary. You may decline, and your child
may stop at any point, for any reason, with no consequence. If your child stops partway through,
their data up to that point is deleted rather than analysed, unless you say otherwise.

**Contact:** Questions about the study → [researcher email]. Questions about your rights as a
participant's guardian → [supervisor email, or ethics committee contact if one exists].

## Part B — Student Assent Form (plain-language version, read to or by the student)

**What's happening:** We'd like you to try a short chemistry activity on a tablet/computer, with
a short quiz before and after. It's to help check whether a new part of the app notices the kinds
of mix-ups students sometimes make with acids and bases — not to test or grade you.

**You don't have to do this.** Even if a parent/guardian already said yes, it's still your choice.
You can say no, or stop partway through, and nothing bad happens either way — it won't affect
your marks or how anyone sees you at school.

**What we'll look at:** the steps you take while doing the practical in the app, and your answers
on the two short quizzes. We won't use your name — just a code, like "P07."

**Questions?** Ask [researcher name] any time, before, during, or after.
